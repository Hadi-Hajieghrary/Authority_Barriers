\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec:problem}

Quadrotor $i\in\{1,\dots,N\}$ has position $p_i\in\R^3$, mass $m_i$, and thrust vector $u_i:=f_iR_ie_3$, where $f_i\ge0$ is the thrust magnitude, $R_i\in SO(3)$ the attitude, and $e_3$ the vertical unit vector; an attitude controller produces the commanded thrust vectors (Remark~\ref{rem:inner}). Each thrust vector lies in the ball $\mathcal{F}_i:=\{u\in\R^3:\ \|u\|\le f_{\max,i}\}$.

The payload is a point mass $m_L$ at $x_L\in\R^3$, attached to quadrotor $i$ by a massless, inextensible cable of length $l_i$ with unit direction $q_i\in\Sph$ from the payload to the quadrotor, $p_i=x_L+l_iq_i$. The cable exerts the force $T_iq_i$ on the payload and $-T_iq_i$ on the quadrotor, with $T_i\ge0$ because a cable cannot push. With the gravitational acceleration $g$,
\begin{align}
m_L\ddot x_L &= \textstyle\sum_{i} T_i q_i - m_L g e_3, \label{eq:load}\\
m_i\ddot p_i &= u_i - m_i g e_3 - T_i q_i. \label{eq:veh}
\end{align}
The state is $x:=(x_L,\dot x_L,q,\dot q)$ with $q:=(q_1,\dots,q_N)$ and $(q_i,\dot q_i)\in T\Sph:=\{(q,\omega)\in\Sph\times\R^3:\ q\tr\omega=0\}$ for every $i$. Thus $q_i\tr q_i=1$ and $q_i\tr\dot q_i=0$ are part of the state manifold. We assume every cable is taut, with tension at least $T_{\min}>0$; the filter in Sec.~\ref{sec:filter} enforces this bound, and the simulations model slack cables.

We decompose each thrust vector along the cable and perpendicular to it, $u_i=s_iq_i+u_i^{\perp}$ with $s_i:=q_i\tr u_i$, $u_i^\perp:=P_iu_i$, $P_i:=I-q_iq_i\tr$, and we use the \emph{specific force} of the payload, $a:=\ddot x_L+ge_3$.

\begin{lemma}[Structure]\label{lem:structure}
Along any solution of \eqref{eq:load}--\eqref{eq:veh} with taut cables:
(a) $T_i = s_i - m_i\,q_i\tr a + m_i l_i\|\dot q_i\|^2$, so the tension depends on the inputs only through the parallel components $s=(s_1,\dots,s_N)$;
(b) $\ddot q_i = \tfrac{1}{m_il_i}u_i^\perp - \tfrac{1}{l_i}P_i a - \|\dot q_i\|^2 q_i$, so the cable direction depends on the inputs only through $u_i^\perp$ and $a$;
(c) $M_a(q)\,a=\sum_i s_iq_i+\sum_i m_il_i\|\dot q_i\|^2q_i$ with $M_a(q):=m_LI+\sum_i m_iq_iq_i\tr\succ0$, so the inputs determine $a$, and by (a) the tensions, uniquely and affinely.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate $p_i=x_L+l_iq_i$ twice and substitute \eqref{eq:load}--\eqref{eq:veh}. Since $q_i\tr\dot q_i=0$ and $q_i\tr\ddot q_i=-\|\dot q_i\|^2$, the projection of \eqref{eq:veh} along $q_i$ gives (a) and the projection perpendicular to $q_i$ gives (b); substituting (a) into \eqref{eq:load} gives (c).
\end{proof}

In words, the parallel component sets the tension, the perpendicular component swings the cable, the two share the limit $\|s_iq_i+u_i^\perp\|\le f_{\max,i}$, and the quadrotors interact only through the payload's specific force. For fixed $(q,\dot q)$, (a) and (c) define an affine bijection between $s$ and $T=(T_1,\dots,T_N)$, so we use the tensions as decision variables: given $T$, $a=\frac{1}{m_L}\sum_jT_jq_j$, and (a) returns $s_i(T)$.

\subsection{Constraints, viability, and the problem}

We consider a wall with horizontal unit normal $n$ pointing into it, with the constraint
\begin{equation}
h(x_L):=d_0-n\tr x_L\ge 0,
\label{eq:h}
\end{equation}
where $h$ is the distance from the payload to the wall. We write $v:=n\tr\dot x_L$ for the approach speed, $y:=-n$ for the braking direction, and $b:=y\tr\ddot x_L$ for the braking deceleration, so that $\dot h=-v$ and $\dot v=-b$, and we describe each cable direction by the \emph{swing coordinates} $z_i:=q_i\tr y$ and $w_i:=q_i\tr r$, $r:=e_3\times y$. A cable with $z_i>0$ lies in the \emph{braking half-space}: it leans away from the wall and pulls the payload away from it; a cable with $z_i<0$ pulls the payload toward the wall.

We impose the operational constraints
\begin{equation}
\Xop:=\{x:\ e_3\tr q_i\ge\cos\theta_q,\ |z_i|\le\bar z,\ (w_i,\dot w_i)\in\mathcal V(\nu_w,\bar w),\ \|\dot q_i\|\le\bar\omega\ \forall i\},
\label{eq:Xop}
\end{equation}
with $\bar z^2+\bar w^2\le\sin^2\theta_q$, $\theta_q<\pi/2$. The upper-cone condition $e_3\tr q_i\ge\cos\theta_q$, together with the bounds on $(z_i,w_i)$, keeps every cable within $\theta_q$ of the upward vertical; the remaining conditions reserve stopping room for the transverse swing and bound the swing rate. The set $\mathcal V$ is defined in \eqref{eq:box} below. With the altitude $\chi:=e_3\tr x_L$ of the payload and a band $[\chi_{\min},\chi_{\max}]$, the constraint set is $\mathcal{C}:=\{x\in\Xop:\ h\ge 0,\ \chi_{\min}\le\chi\le\chi_{\max}\}$. The inputs must respect the thrust limits, the tension bound, and a bound $a_{\max}$ on the payload's specific force:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{U}(x):=\{u:\ &u_i\in\mathcal{F}_i,\ T_i(x,u)\ge T_{\min}\ \forall i,\\ &\textstyle\|\sum_iT_i(x,u)q_i\|\le m_La_{\max}\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:U}
\end{equation}
The last constraint is a design limit on the payload's acceleration; the bound that follows from the thrust limits alone is too large for Assumption~\ref{ass:split} to hold with realistic constants.

A state is \emph{viable} if some input signal with values in $\mathcal{U}(x)$ keeps the trajectory in $\mathcal{C}$ forever; the set of viable states is the \emph{viability kernel} $\Viab(\mathcal{C})$ \cite{aubin1991viability}, outside of which no controller can keep the system safe. A set is \emph{controlled invariant} if some admissible input signal keeps every trajectory that starts in it inside it; every controlled-invariant subset of $\mathcal{C}$ lies in $\Viab(\mathcal{C})$, which has no closed form. The problem is: (a) find a controlled-invariant set $\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{C}$ whose membership can be tested online; (b) find an explicit set that contains $\Viab(\mathcal{C})$; and (c) derive a safety filter that is feasible at every state of $\mathcal{S}$.
