\section{Numerical study}\label{sec:sim}
Figures~\ref{fig:profile} and \ref{fig:trial-distances}--\ref{fig:trial-phase} illustrate the predicted and realized swing profiles. The full-order simulation has four $1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ vehicles carrying a $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ point payload on $1\,\mathrm m$ cables. It uses $T_{\min}=1\,\mathrm N$, $f_{\max}=44\,\mathrm N$, $\bar T_i=3.2\,\mathrm N$, $\rho_i=34\,\mathrm N$, $a_{\max}=13\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, $\bar z=0.239$, $\bar w=0.171$, $\bar\omega=1\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, and $\nu=\nu_w=0.5\,\mathrm{s^{-2}}$. Figure~\ref{fig:thrust} shows the thrust split. The thrust-reserve bound is arithmetically consistent: $\sqrt{(\bar T_i+m_ia_{\max}+m_il_i\bar\omega^2)^2+\rho_i^2}=41.73\,\mathrm N<(f_{\max}\mathrm = 44 N)$. Its spring--damper cables can go slack; the filter runs at $200\,\mathrm{Hz}$.

All 690 HOCBF trials over the tested gain pairs and actuator modes lost QP feasibility before a cable entered the braking half-space (Fig.~\ref{fig:e1}); this does not establish initial feasibility for arbitrary gains. Of 100 trials started close to $H=0$ under an adversarial nominal input, the sampled authority filter reached the wall in 59 despite a feasible QP at its sampling instants (Fig.~\ref{fig:sampled}). In 56 trials the spring--damper cables temporarily lost the positive-tension condition. Thus the continuous-time invariant-set theorem does not certify these runs. The largest barrier loss was $0.85\,\mathrm m$ within one $5\,\mathrm{ms}$ hold; no contact was observed above an initial margin of $0.78\,\mathrm m$ in the tested 100-state sweep. This margin is empirical and holds for the tested distribution only. A $1\,\mathrm{kHz}$ attitude loop also incurred $17$--$43\,\mathrm N$ tracking errors during command jumps.

Direct-collocation searches covered three initial cable configurations at eight speeds, with each candidate subsequently simulated. When cables already lean away from the wall, the observed feasible distance is about $0.97$--$0.98$ times $\Dres$; when a swing is required, feasible trajectories can begin at only $0.25$--$0.55\,\Dres$ (Figs.~\ref{fig:sandwich-a} and~\ref{fig:sandwich-bc}). Figure~\ref{fig:sequence} shows one of the found trajectories. The large gap when cables lean toward the wall is driven in part by the reserved swing acceleration: $\nu=0.5\,\mathrm{s^{-2}}$ needs about $1.8\,\mathrm{s}$ to cross the allowed angle range, while the physical thrust in the simulations can reach the swing-rate limit much sooner. Figure~\ref{fig:nu} varies a hypothetical $\nu$ to show this sensitivity; values beyond the shaded admissible region cannot be substituted into the theorem without changing its assumptions. A collocation success gives an example trajectory but a failure does not locate the viability boundary. The numerical evidence therefore supports the qualitative gap between the reserved and relaxed models, while it cannot validate either set inclusion or a universal percentage gap.

Evaluating $\Dres$ takes $0.5$--$0.6\,\mathrm{ms}$ and the filter $2$--$4.5\,\mathrm{ms}$, close to the $5\,\mathrm{ms}$ sampling period; solver failures, model mismatch, and inter-sample excursions preclude a real-time guarantee. A certified zero-order-hold error bound and an explicit rate constraint are needed before claims of sampled safety are made.
