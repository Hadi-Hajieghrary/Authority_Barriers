\section{Introduction}
A quadrotor can pull a cable-suspended payload but cannot push it. If all vehicles are between a moving payload and a wall, changing the tensions cannot brake the payload; a vehicle first has to swing to its far side. This delay is hidden by a high-order control barrier function (HOCBF) that tests only the currently achievable acceleration. We quantify the delay and construct a safety certificate from the maximum wallward excursion of a feasible braking maneuver.

Input-constrained HOCBFs~\cite{xiao2022high,breeden2021high} and backup CBFs~\cite{chen2021backup,vanwijk2025} address feasibility through different safe sets. Cable-robot wrench calculations describe forces available at a fixed geometry~\cite{gouttefarde2011interval,erskine2019wrench}; aerial transport models describe how that geometry moves~\cite{sreenath2013dynamics,lee2018geometric}. Here the cable directions determine a time-varying tension-force zonotope, and we use a realizable swing-and-brake maneuver to construct an explicit inner bound and a relaxed swing model for an outer bound on viability. Closed-form backup control is also studied in~\cite{vanwijk2025}; our closed form concerns the future wall excursion for this particular authority model.

We let each vehicle reserve part of its thrust for the cable tension and part for swinging the cable; the braking authority that this guarantees depends on the cable directions alone (Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}). When every cable swings to its limit in minimum time, the payload covers at most the stopping distance $\Dres$ toward the wall, which is computed exactly (Sec.~\ref{sec:barrier}). The authority barrier is $H:=h-\Dres$: the states with $H\ge0$ and admissible swing states form a controlled-invariant set, on which the filter of Sec.~\ref{sec:filter} always has an admissible input. With upper bounds on the authority and on the swing acceleration, the same calculation gives an optimistic stopping distance $\Drel$, below which no state is viable (Theorem~\ref{thm:sandwich}). Both bounds hold for every system whose braking authority is bounded by nondecreasing functions of coordinates that the inputs drive only through their second derivative (Sec.~\ref{sec:class}); the cable system is one instance.

The contributions are: (i) a precise condition under which an initially feasible HOCBF filter must lose feasibility before a cable reaches the braking half-space; (ii) a continuous-time, input-constrained authority barrier and an outer viability bound; and (iii) simulations that measure both the bound's conservatism and the substantial failure of a direct sampled implementation. The latter concerns the implementation and gives no safety guarantee. In simulation, the HOCBF filter lost feasibility in all 690 trials, and the sampled authority filter reached the wall in 59 of 100 trials started near the boundary of its safe set.

For the two-vehicle example in Fig.~\ref{fig:scene}, $m_L=m_i=l_i=1$ in SI units, each cable leans $30^\circ$ toward the wall, and $T_{\min}=1\,\mathrm{N}$. At $h=1\,\mathrm m$ and $v=0.85\,\mathrm{m/s}$, a linear HOCBF with gains $(1,20)\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ admits the minimum tensions, but its safe-set condition tightens before a cable can reach the vertical at the allowed $2\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ swing rate. Section~\ref{sec:negative} gives the exact conditional statement.
