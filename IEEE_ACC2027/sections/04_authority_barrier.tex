\section{The Authority Barrier}\label{sec:theory}

To obtain a braking authority that depends only on the cable directions, we fix in advance how each quadrotor divides its thrust between tension and swinging. Throughout, $\pos{z}:=\max\{0,z\}$ and $\negp{z}:=\max\{0,-z\}$.

\begin{assumption}[Thrust reserve]\label{ass:split}
There exist constants $\bar T_i\ge T_{\min}$ (tension limit) and $\rho_i\ge 0$ (swing reserve) with $\sum_j\bar T_j\le m_La_{\max}$ such that, for every $x\in\Xop$, every $T$ with $T_{\min}\le T_j\le\bar T_j$ for all $j$, and every $u_i^\perp\perp q_i$ with $\|u_i^\perp\|\le\rho_i$, the thrust vector $s_i(T)q_i+u_i^\perp$ lies in $\mathcal{F}_i$.
\end{assumption}

In words, each quadrotor can produce any tension up to $\bar T_i$ and, simultaneously, any perpendicular thrust up to $\rho_i$, and such tensions respect the cone in \eqref{eq:U}; by Lemma~\ref{lem:structure}(a), the assumption holds if $(\bar T_i+m_ia_{\max}+m_il_i\bar\omega^2)^2+\rho_i^2\le f_{\max,i}^2$.

\begin{definition}[Directional authority]\label{def:authority}
Let the \emph{hover floors} $T^{\mathrm h}_i\in[T_{\min},\bar T_i]$ satisfy $\cos\theta_q\sum_iT^{\mathrm h}_i\ge m_Lg$, and let $\phi_i(z):=\bar T_i\pos{z}-T^{\mathrm h}_i\negp{z}$. The \emph{directional authority} of $q$ along the braking direction $y$ is
\begin{equation}
\alpha_y(q):=\frac{1}{m_L}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi_i(z_i),\qquad z_i=q_i\tr y.
\label{eq:authority}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

A cable in the braking half-space contributes most at $\bar T_i$, a cable that leans toward the wall opposes least at its floor; each $\phi_i$ is nondecreasing, convex, and piecewise linear with one breakpoint. The floor is $T^{\mathrm h}_i$ and not $T_{\min}$ because tensions at the floors carry the payload for every cable configuration of $\Xop$: a cable that leans toward the wall and carries its share of the weight pushes the payload toward the wall, and the authority charges this push.

\begin{lemma}[Authority as a support function]\label{lem:support}
The set $\mathcal{Z}(q):=\{\sum_iT_iq_i:\ T^{\mathrm h}_i\le T_i\le\bar T_i\}$ of tension forces is a zonotope, and $m_L\alpha_y(q)=\max_{f\in\mathcal{Z}(q)}y\tr f$ is its support function along $y$. Under Assumption~\ref{ass:split}, every $b\in[-\alpha_{-y}(q),\alpha_y(q)]$ is produced by some input in $\mathcal{U}(x)$ at every $x\in\Xop$; hence $\alpha_y\le\alpha^{\mathrm{ex}}_y$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\mathcal{Z}(q)$ is the Minkowski sum of the segments $[T^{\mathrm h}_i,\bar T_i]\,q_i$, the support function of a Minkowski sum is the sum of the support functions, and $\max_{T\in[T^{\mathrm h}_i,\bar T_i]}T\,z_i=\phi_i(z_i)$. Assumption~\ref{ass:split} with $u_i^\perp=0$ realizes every $T$ in the box, and $b=\frac{1}{m_L}\sum_iT_iz_i$ is affine in $T$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Systems with second-order authority}\label{sec:class}

The construction uses two properties of the cable system, stated here for a general system with coordinates $\zeta_1,\dots,\zeta_N$: the braking authority is bounded above and below by nondecreasing functions of the coordinates, and each coordinate responds to the inputs only through its second derivative. For $\nu,\bar\zeta>0$, the \emph{admissible swing set}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{V}(\nu,\bar\zeta):=\{(\zeta,\omega)\in\R^2:\ -\bar\zeta+\tfrac{\negp{\omega}^2}{2\nu}\le\zeta\le\bar\zeta-\tfrac{\pos{\omega}^2}{2\nu}\}
\label{eq:box}
\end{equation}
is the convex set of $(\zeta,\dot\zeta)$ from which a double integrator with $|\ddot\zeta|\le\nu$ can keep $|\zeta|\le\bar\zeta$ for all future time.

\begin{definition}[Second-order authority]\label{def:class}
Consider a system with admissible inputs, a constraint function $h$ with $\dot h=-v$ and $\dot v=-b$, a closed operational set $\Xop$, and twice continuously differentiable coordinates $\zeta_1,\dots,\zeta_N$ of the state. The system has \emph{second-order authority} along $h$ on $\Xop$ with data $(\ua,\oa,\nu,\nu^{\mathrm{rel}},\bar\zeta)$ if:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(C1)] $\ua$ is the sum of a constant and of $N$ continuous, nondecreasing, piecewise-linear functions of the individual $\zeta_i$; $\oa\ge\ua$ is continuous and nondecreasing in each $\zeta_i$; and $b\le\oa(\zeta)$ for every admissible input at every $x\in\Xop$.
\item[(C2)] $|\zeta_i|\le\bar\zeta$ on $\Xop$, and $|\ddot\zeta_i|\le\nu^{\mathrm{rel}}$ for every admissible input at every $x\in\Xop$.
\item[(C3)] There is a map that assigns to every $x\in\Xop$ with $(\zeta_i,\dot\zeta_i)\in\mathcal{V}(\nu,\bar\zeta)$ for all $i$, and to every choice of $\ddot\zeta^{\mathrm{d}}_i\in[-\nu,\nu]$, an admissible input that produces $b=\ua(\zeta)$ and $\ddot\zeta_i=\ddot\zeta^{\mathrm{d}}_i$ for all $i$, and whose solutions remain in $\Xop$ as long as $(\zeta_i,\dot\zeta_i)\in\mathcal{V}(\nu,\bar\zeta)$ for all $i$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

In words, no admissible input brakes harder than $\oa$ or swings faster than $\nu^{\mathrm{rel}}$, while a reserved part of the input swings every coordinate at any rate up to $\nu$ and brakes with $\ua$ at the same time. For such a system the wall constraint alone defines $\mathcal{C}_h:=\{x\in\Xop:\ h\ge0\}$.

\subsection{The braking maneuver and its stopping distance}\label{sec:barrier}

Fix a deceleration $\nu'\in[\nu/2,\nu)$. For $(\zeta,\omega)\in\mathcal{V}(\nu,\bar\zeta)$, let $\tilde\zeta(t;\zeta,\omega)$ be the motion from $(\zeta,\omega)$ to $(\bar\zeta,0)$ that accelerates at $\nu$ and decelerates at $\nu'$, held at $\bar\zeta$ afterwards: with $r:=\sqrt{(2\nu\nu'(\bar\zeta-\zeta)+\nu'\omega^2)/(\nu+\nu')}$, $t_1:=(r-\omega)/\nu$, and $t_2:=t_1+r/\nu'$,
\begin{equation}
\tilde\zeta(t)=\begin{cases}
\zeta+\omega t+\tfrac{\nu}{2}t^2, & 0\le t\le t_1,\\
\bar\zeta-\tfrac{\nu'}{2}(t_2-t)^2, & t_1\le t\le t_2,\\
\bar\zeta, & t\ge t_2,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:profile}
\end{equation}
if $\omega\le0$ or $\omega^2<2\nu'(\bar\zeta-\zeta)$; otherwise, the motion decelerates from the start at the constant rate $\omega^2/(2(\bar\zeta-\zeta))\in[\nu',\nu]$ and arrives at $t_2=2(\bar\zeta-\zeta)/\omega$. Because $\nu'<\nu$, the swing arrives through the interior of $\mathcal V(\nu,\bar\zeta)$; the time-optimal swing, $\nu'=\nu$, is denoted $\tilde\zeta^{\circ}$. The swing is continuously differentiable in its initial data except on the curve $\omega^2=2\nu'(\bar\zeta-\zeta)$, $\omega\ge0$, where its two cases meet, and its derivatives are one-sided. A state is \emph{regular} if none of its swing states lies on this curve, and \emph{degenerate} otherwise.

Let $x$ have $(\zeta_i,\dot\zeta_i)\in\mathcal{V}(\nu,\bar\zeta)$ for all $i$, and let every coordinate follow its swing $\tilde\zeta_i(t):=\tilde\zeta(t;\zeta_i,\dot\zeta_i)$. Along the maneuver, the braking deceleration is $\tilde\alpha(t;x):=\ua(\tilde\zeta_1(t),\dots,\tilde\zeta_N(t))$, the approach speed $\tilde v(t;x):=v-\int_0^t\tilde\alpha(s;x)\,ds$, and the distance traveled toward the wall $E(t;x):=\int_0^t\tilde v(s;x)\,ds$. The \emph{stopping distance} is
\begin{equation}
\Dres(x):=\max_{t\ge0}E(t;x),
\label{eq:D}
\end{equation}
with maximizing times $T^\star(x)$; the maximum exists if $\ua_{\mathrm{sat}}:=\ua(\bar\zeta,\dots,\bar\zeta)>0$, and it is taken over all $t\ge0$, not up to the first zero of $\tilde v$, because a negative authority accelerates a payload at rest toward the wall. The \emph{authority barrier} is $H(x):=h-\Dres(x)$, and
\begin{equation}
\Xrf:=\{x\in\Xop:\ H(x)\ge0,\ (\zeta_i,\dot\zeta_i)\in\mathcal{V}(\nu,\bar\zeta)\ \forall i\}.
\label{eq:Xrf}
\end{equation}
$H(x)$ is the smallest distance to the wall that the payload reaches during the maneuver started at $x$. The functions $h^{+}_i:=\bar\zeta-\zeta_i-\pos{\dot\zeta_i}^2/(2\nu)$ and $h^{-}_i:=\bar\zeta+\zeta_i-\negp{\dot\zeta_i}^2/(2\nu)$ are nonnegative exactly on $\mathcal V(\nu,\bar\zeta)$. For the swing-set barrier condition below, define the signed square root $\operatorname{sroot}(s):=\operatorname{sgn}(s)\sqrt{|s|}$, with $\operatorname{sroot}(0):=0$; on $s\ge0$ this is the ordinary square root. Let $\plan(x)$ be the input that (C3) assigns to $x$ with $\ddot\zeta^{\mathrm{d}}_i=\ddot{\tilde\zeta}(0;\zeta_i,\dot\zeta_i)$; since the swing is a feedback of $(\zeta_i,\dot\zeta_i)$, the closed loop under $\plan$ reproduces the swings, $b(t)=\tilde\alpha(t;x_0)$, and $v(t)=\tilde v(t;x_0)$.

\begin{lemma}[Envelope and regularity]\label{lem:envelope}
(i) Every $\zeta(\cdot)$ with absolutely continuous derivative, $\zeta(0)=\zeta$, $\dot\zeta(0)=\omega$, $|\ddot\zeta|\le\nu$ a.e., and $\zeta\le\bar\zeta$ satisfies $\zeta(t)\le\tilde\zeta^{\circ}(t;\zeta,\omega)$ for all $t\ge0$. (ii) $\Dres$ is locally Lipschitz on the states with $(\zeta_i,\dot\zeta_i)\in\mathcal V(\nu,\bar\zeta)$ for all $i$, and along the maneuver started at $x_0$, $\Dres(x(t))=\max_{s\ge t}\int_0^s\tilde v(\cdot;x_0)-\int_0^t\tilde v(\cdot;x_0)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof scheme]
(i) Write $S(\zeta):=\sqrt{2\nu(\bar\zeta-\zeta)}$. A trajectory moving toward $\bar\zeta$ must satisfy $\dot\zeta\le S(\zeta)$, or even full braking would overshoot. Before its switch, $\tilde\zeta^{\circ}$ uses the largest acceleration $\nu$; afterwards it follows $\dot{\tilde\zeta}{}^{\circ}=S(\tilde\zeta^{\circ})$ to $\bar\zeta$. A first crossing from below either contradicts maximal acceleration before the switch or forces both motions to coincide on the stopping curve after it. (ii) On compact initial-state sets the arrival times and the maximizing times are uniformly bounded because $\ua_{\rm sat}>0$, and $E(t;\cdot)$ is locally Lipschitz uniformly in $t$; the maximum of such a family is locally Lipschitz. Restarting the swing at time $t$ reproduces its remaining trajectory, which gives the time-shift identity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Main result}\label{sec:main}

At regular states, for fixed $t$, $E(t;x)$ is continuously differentiable in $(v,\zeta,\dot\zeta)$, because the integration over $s$ smooths the breakpoints of $\ua$, which each $\tilde\zeta_i$ meets only at isolated times except while held at $\bar\zeta$. There, Danskin's theorem \cite{clarke1990optimization} gives the directional derivative
\begin{equation}
\Dres'(x;\delta)=\max_{t\in T^\star(x)}\nabla_xE(t;x)\tr\delta
\label{eq:dirder}
\end{equation}
in directions $\delta$ along which the state stays in the domain of $\Dres$. For an admissible $u$, let $F_u(x)$ be the state derivative and let $H'(x;F_u(x))$ be the upper right Dini derivative of $H$ along it; at regular states it equals $-v-\Dres'(x;F_u(x))$, the ordinary derivative $\dot H(x,u)$ when the maximizing time is unique. On states with all $(\zeta_i,\dot\zeta_i)\in\mathcal{V}(\nu^{\mathrm{rel}},\bar\zeta)$, let $\Drel$ be \eqref{eq:D} computed with $(\oa,\nu^{\mathrm{rel}})$ in place of $(\ua,\nu)$ and with the time-optimal swing: the stopping distance of an optimistic system that swings and brakes as hard as any admissible input allows. Let $\gamma$ be a locally Lipschitz extended class-$\mathcal{K}$ function.

\begin{theorem}[Inner and outer bounds]\label{thm:sandwich}
Let the system have second-order authority along $h$ on $\Xop$ with data $(\ua,\oa,\nu,\nu^{\mathrm{rel}},\bar\zeta)$, and let $\ua_{\mathrm{sat}}>0$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\Xrf$ is controlled invariant. For every $x\in\Xrf$, the set $K_H(x)$ of the admissible $u$ with $H'(x;F_u(x))\ge-\gamma(H(x))$ and $\dot h^{\pm}_i\ge-c_V\operatorname{sroot}(h^{\pm}_i)$ for all $i$, $c_V:=2\sqrt{2\nu\nu'/(\nu-\nu')}$, contains $\plan(x)$. Every solution in $\Xop$ generated by a measurable selection $u(t)\in K_H(x(t))$ from $x(0)\in\Xrf$ satisfies $(\zeta_i,\dot\zeta_i)\in\mathcal{V}(\nu,\bar\zeta)$ and $h(t)\ge H(x(t))\ge0$ for all $t\ge0$.
\item[(ii)] $\Viab(\mathcal{C}_h)\subseteq\{x\in\Xop:\ (\zeta_i,\dot\zeta_i)\in\mathcal{V}(\nu^{\mathrm{rel}},\bar\zeta)\ \forall i,\ h\ge\Drel(x)\}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) Under $\plan$ from $x_0\in\Xrf$, Lemma~\ref{lem:envelope}(ii) and $h(t)=h_0-\int_0^tv$ give $H(x(t))=h_0-\max_{s\ge t}\int_0^s v$, nondecreasing in $t$; $\plan$ keeps the state in $\Xop$ and the swing states in $\mathcal{V}$, so $\Xrf$ is forward invariant under $\plan$, hence controlled invariant. Next, the swing restarted at a later state of the maneuver is the remaining part of the swing started at $x$, so the derivative of $E(t;\cdot)$ along $F_{\plan}(x)$ equals $\tilde v(t;x)-v$ at regular states, and by \eqref{eq:dirder}, $H'(x;F_{\plan}(x))=-\max_{t\in T^\star(x)}\tilde v(t;x)$. Every maximizing time $t>0$ is a stationary point of $E(\cdot;x)$, so $\tilde v(t;x)=0$, and $t=0$ maximizes only if $v\le0$; hence $H'(x;F_{\plan}(x))\ge0\ge-\gamma(H(x))$ at regular states. The time-shift identity gives the same inequality for the Dini derivative along $\plan$ at degenerate states. The swing of $\plan$ satisfies the conditions on $h^{\pm}_i$: $\dot h^{-}_i\ge0$; while it accelerates with $\dot\zeta_i>0$, $\dot h^{+}_i=-2\dot\zeta_i$ and $\dot\zeta_i^2<2\nu'(\bar\zeta-\zeta_i)<2\nu\nu'h^{+}_i/(\nu-\nu')$; while it decelerates at a rate $a\in[\nu',\nu]$, $(\dot h^{+}_i)^2=2a(1-a/\nu)h^{+}_i\le2(\nu-\nu')h^{+}_i\le c_V^2h^{+}_i$. Finally, along a solution generated by $K_H$, the swing states remain in $\mathcal V$. Indeed, if a connected component $(t_1,t_2)$ of $\{t:h_i^{\pm}(t)<0\}$ existed, continuity would give $h_i^{\pm}(t_1)=0$, while on $(t_1,t_2)$ the signed extension satisfies $\operatorname{sroot}(h_i^{\pm})=-\sqrt{-h_i^{\pm}}$ and hence $\dot h_i^{\pm}\ge c_V\sqrt{-h_i^{\pm}}>0$ a.e.; absolute continuity would then imply $h_i^{\pm}(t)\ge h_i^{\pm}(t_1)=0$, a contradiction. Thus the swing states stay in $\mathcal{V}$, where $\Dres$ is locally Lipschitz, so $H(x(t))$ is absolutely continuous with $H'(x;\dot x)\ge-\gamma(H)$ a.e., and the comparison argument for nonsmooth barriers \cite{glotfelter2017nonsmooth} gives $H\ge0$; $h\ge H$ since $\Dres\ge0$.

(ii) Let $x_0\in\Viab(\mathcal{C}_h)$ and let an admissible input keep the solution in $\mathcal{C}_h$. By (C2), $(\zeta_i,\dot\zeta_i)\in\mathcal{V}(\nu^{\mathrm{rel}},\bar\zeta)$ at all times, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:envelope}(i) with $\nu^{\mathrm{rel}}$, $\zeta_i(t)\le\tilde\zeta_i^{\mathrm{rel}}(t)$. By (C1) and the monotonicity of $\oa$, $b(t)\le\oa(\zeta(t))\le\oa(\tilde\zeta^{\mathrm{rel}}(t))=\tilde\alpha^{\mathrm{rel}}(t;x_0)$, so $v(t)\ge\tilde v^{\mathrm{rel}}(t;x_0)$ and $\min_th(t)\le h_0-\Drel(x_0)$; if $h_0<\Drel(x_0)$, the solution leaves $\mathcal{C}_h$.
\end{proof}

Together, (i) and (ii) give $\Xrf\subseteq\Viab(\mathcal{C}_h)\subseteq\{h\ge\Drel\}$. The gap $\Dres-\Drel$ is what the division of the input costs; for a constant authority $\ua\equiv\oa\equiv\bar\alpha>0$ both distances equal $\pos{v}^2/(2\bar\alpha)$, the braking-distance barrier of \cite{ames2014control}. Part (i) is a backup-CBF guarantee \cite{gurriet2020scalable,chen2021backup} with $\plan$ as the backup controller, but the closest approach along the backup trajectory has the closed form \eqref{eq:D} instead of being simulated, and the same data yield the outer bound (ii). The sets $\Xrf$ and $\Cho$ need not be nested.

\subsection{Application to the cable system}\label{sec:cable}

\begin{assumption}[Swing reserve]\label{ass:nu}
There exist $\nu,\nu_w>0$ and $\rho_i^{\mathrm{fb}}\ge0$ such that, for every $i$, $\sec\theta_q\sqrt{\bar\eta_{z,i}^2+\bar\eta_{w,i}^2}+\rho_i^{\mathrm{fb}}\le\rho_i$, where $\bar\eta_{z,i}:=m_i(l_i\nu+a_{\max}+l_i\bar\omega^2\bar z)$ and $\bar\eta_{w,i}:=m_i(l_i\nu_w+a_{\max}+l_i\bar\omega^2\bar w)$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Swing-rate consistency]\label{ass:op}
$\bar\omega^2\ge\sec^2\theta_q\,(4\nu\bar z+4\nu_w\bar w)$.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:nu} reserves, within $\rho_i$, the perpendicular thrust needed to impose any $\ddot z_i\in[-\nu,\nu]$ and $\ddot w_i\in[-\nu_w,\nu_w]$ against the coupling terms of Lemma~\ref{lem:structure}(b), the factor $\sec\theta_q$ accounting for the non-orthogonality of the two swing directions on the sphere, and keeps $\rho_i^{\mathrm{fb}}$ for feedback; Assumption~\ref{ass:op} makes the swing-rate bound of \eqref{eq:Xop} hold on the admissible swing sets.

The maneuver also bounds the altitude. Its tensions lie in $[T^{\mathrm h}_i,\bar T_i]$, so the vertical acceleration $\ddot\chi=\frac{1}{m_L}\sum_iT_ie_3\tr q_i-g$ lies between $c_0:=\frac{\cos\theta_q}{m_L}\sum_iT^{\mathrm h}_i-g\ge0$ and $c_1:=\frac{1}{m_L}\sum_i\bar T_i-g$ until the \emph{stopping time} $t_s(x):=\max\{t_a+\tilde v(t_a;x)/\ua_{\mathrm{sat}},\ t_a-\epsilon,\ 0\}$, $t_a:=\max_it_{2,i}$, with a lead $\epsilon>0$ small enough that $z_i>0$ whenever $t_{2,i}\le\epsilon$; at $t_s$ every swing is about to arrive and the payload recedes from the wall. From then on $\plan$ holds the altitude with one uniform tension that decelerates a climb at some $c_\downarrow>0$ satisfying $c_\downarrow\le g-\frac{N}{m_L}T_{\min}$ and a descent at some $c_\uparrow>0$ satisfying $c_\uparrow\le\frac{\cos\theta_q}{m_L}\sum_i\bar T_i-g$. With $U:=\dot\chi+c_1t_s$ and $L:=\dot\chi+c_0t_s$, let $\Delta_\uparrow:=\pos{\dot\chi t_s+\tfrac{c_1}{2}t_s^2+\tfrac{U^2}{2c_\downarrow}}$ if $U>0$ and $\Delta_\uparrow:=0$ otherwise; let $\Delta_\downarrow:=0$ if $\dot\chi\ge0$, $\Delta_\downarrow:=\dot\chi^2/(2c_0)$ if $\dot\chi<0\le L$, and $\Delta_\downarrow:=-\dot\chi t_s-\tfrac{c_0}{2}t_s^2+\tfrac{L^2}{2c_\uparrow}$ if $L<0$. The \emph{altitude barriers} are
\[
H_\uparrow:=\chi_{\max}-\chi-\Delta_\uparrow,\qquad H_\downarrow:=\chi-\chi_{\min}-\Delta_\downarrow,
\]
and the \emph{certified set} is $\mathcal{S}:=\Xrf\cap\{H_\uparrow\ge0,\ H_\downarrow\ge0\}$. Because $t_s$, $\Delta_\uparrow$, and $\Delta_\downarrow$ are defined by maxima and piecewise formulas, the altitude barriers are generally only piecewise differentiable. Below, $\dot H_\uparrow$ and $\dot H_\downarrow$ denote the ordinary derivatives on smooth branches; at switching surfaces the inequalities are understood for every active one-sided branch (and hence in the corresponding upper-right Dini sense).

\begin{proposition}[The cable system has second-order authority]\label{prop:instance}
Let Assumptions~\ref{ass:split}, \ref{ass:nu}, and \ref{ass:op} hold and let $\zeta_i:=z_i$. Then \eqref{eq:load}--\eqref{eq:veh} has second-order authority along $h$ on $\Xop$ with $\ua=\alpha_y$ of \eqref{eq:authority},
\begin{align}
\oa(z)=\min\Big\{\tfrac{1}{m_L}\textstyle\sum_i&\phi^{\mathrm{rel}}_i(z_i),\label{eq:oa}\\
 &a_{\max}\sec\theta_q\,\max_i\pos{z_i}\Big\},\nonumber
\end{align}
where $\phi^{\mathrm{rel}}_i$ is $\phi_i$ with $\bar T_i^{\mathrm{rel}}:=f_{\max,i}+m_ia_{\max}+m_il_i\bar\omega^2$; $\nu$ of Assumption~\ref{ass:nu}; $\nu^{\mathrm{rel}}:=\max_i(\frac{f_{\max,i}}{m_il_i}+\frac{a_{\max}}{l_i}+\bar\omega^2)$; and $\bar\zeta=\bar z$. The map of (C3) sets $T_i=\bar T_i$ if $z_i>0$ and $T_i=T^{\mathrm h}_i$ otherwise, and steers each $w_i$ within $\mathcal{V}(\nu_w,\bar w)$. Moreover, $\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal C$ is controlled invariant, $\plan$ satisfies $\dot H_\uparrow\ge0$ and $\dot H_\downarrow\ge0$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:sandwich}(i) holds on $\mathcal S$ with these two conditions added to $K_H$.
\end{proposition}

With all cables at $\bar z$, $\Drel=\pos{v}^2/(2a_{\max}\bar z\sec\theta_q)$ against $\Dres=\pos{v}^2/(2\ua_{\mathrm{sat}})$ with $\ua_{\mathrm{sat}}=\frac{1}{m_L}\sum_i\bar T_i\bar z$; where the cables must first swing, the gap is set by $\nu$ against $\nu^{\mathrm{rel}}$.

\begin{proposition}[Computation]\label{prop:decomp}
For $t_j\in T^\star(x)$, the time derivative of $h-E(t_j;\cdot)$ under $u$ is
\begin{align}
\dot H_j(x,u)=-v&+t_j\,b\label{eq:Hdot}\\
&-\sum_{i}\big(\partial_{z_i}E(t_j;x)\,\dot z_i+\partial_{\dot z_i}E(t_j;x)\,\ddot z_i\big),\nonumber
\end{align}
with $b=\frac{1}{m_L}\sum_iT_iz_i$ and $\ddot z_i=\frac{1}{m_il_i}y\tr u_i^\perp-\frac{1}{l_i}y\tr P_ia-\|\dot q_i\|^2z_i$, and $H'(x;F_u(x))=\min_j\dot H_j(x,u)$ at regular states. $\Dres$, $T^\star$, and the coefficients of \eqref{eq:Hdot} depend on the state only through $(v,\{z_i,\dot z_i\}_i)$ and are computed exactly in $O(N\log N)$ arithmetic operations, because $E(\cdot;x)$ is piecewise quartic with at most $4N$ breakpoints; the gradient rows take a further $O(N|T^\star|)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof scheme]
At a regular state, $\partial_vE(t;x)=t$ and the chain rule gives \eqref{eq:Hdot}; Danskin's formula makes the derivative of $H=h-\max_tE(t)$ the minimum over its active times. Every $\tilde z_i$ is piecewise quadratic, with at most its switch, arrival, and two zero crossings as breakpoints. Thus $\tilde\alpha$ is piecewise quadratic, $E$ piecewise quartic, and candidate maximizers are interval endpoints or roots of the cubic $\tilde v$; sorting $O(N)$ events takes $O(N\log N)$ operations.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the guarantee]\label{rem:inner}
The certificate assumes an exact taut-cable model, continuously updated thrust commands, a feasible transverse swing reserve, and the existence of closed-loop solutions. Wind, mass errors, actuator tracking, slack cables, and zero-order-hold inputs require additional error bounds and are \emph{not} covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:sandwich}. In particular, the continuity and local Lipschitzness of $\Dres$ do not, by themselves, give a numerical sampled-data margin.
\end{remark}
